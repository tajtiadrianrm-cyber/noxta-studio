VOEP. A vintage parts shop, built for sale.

Alex runs a vintage outboard engine parts shop out of Belgium, with 4,000+ NOS and used parts in his head and on his shelves, and zero online presence. We took it from "spreadsheet of parts" to a working, payment‑accepting, fully‑indexed store in roughly 60 hours of focused work.

01 / Challenge

4,000+ parts. No website. One owner.

Alex's shop runs on knowledge. Vintage outboard engine parts. Belgian and Dutch hobbyists. A Rolodex of regulars who text or call when they need a magneto cap for a 1962 Johnson. The catalog lived in spreadsheets and on shelves. The internet, until last year, didn't know the shop existed.

The brief was small and large at the same time: be findable, be buyable, look like the thing he actually built. No team to hand it to once it shipped. Photography pulled from his own parts library. Payments built around what a Belgian buyer actually trusts. Bancontact first, iDEAL second, card third.

02 / Approach

WordPress + WooCommerce. Custom child theme. One head, one inbox.

I picked the boring, durable stack on purpose: WordPress + WooCommerce on Hostinger, a custom child theme on top of Flatsome, and the smallest plugin set that could do the job. Nothing in the build relies on a niche tool that might disappear in two years.

The brand identity is intentionally not Noxta. Dark vintage. Ink, brass and rust, pulled from the actual oil‑stained world the parts come from. Type set quiet and authoritative. The customer should feel like they walked into a real workshop, not a SaaS landing page.

For search, FiboSearch by SKU because that's how the customer actually thinks: not "ignition coil" but "584410". Mobile search is hand‑tuned because that's where 80% of orders come from.

  • F / 01

    Custom Flatsome child theme

    Color, type and layout pulled away from the demo. Dark vintage palette of ink, brass and rust, drawn from the parts themselves, not a Pinterest board.

  • F / 02

    FiboSearch by SKU

    Customers type part numbers, not adjectives. Search is tuned for SKU‑first matching with category fallback and a tap‑target sized for one‑handed mobile.

  • F / 03

    Bancontact, iDEAL, card

    WooCommerce Payments configured for the methods Belgian and Dutch buyers actually use first. Card last, not first.

  • F / 05

    Product database upload

    Hours of structured data work. Categories, attributes, SKUs, photography assignment. Boring, slow, the work most builds skip.

  • F / 06

    Mobile‑first search UX

    Sticky search bar, large hit targets, instant results. Tested with a parts list and a phone in one hand, like a customer in a garage.

03 / Result

Live. Selling. Within days.

4,000+
Parts catalogued, structured, searchable by SKU
Days
Between launch and the first paid transaction

The site went live and the first transaction landed within days. Bancontact processed cleanly, no manual chasing. Search was the first thing customers used; SKU‑first matching paid for itself the day a Belgian customer dropped four part numbers into the bar and added them all to cart in one session.

What I'm proudest of is the part of the build that doesn't show: the catalog. Every part has the right category, the right SKU, the right metadata. That's the foundation Alex can keep adding to for years without it falling over. Built so he can run it.
